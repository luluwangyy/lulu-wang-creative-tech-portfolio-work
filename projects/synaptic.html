Overview

How can light, layered materials, and generative sound make the invisible experience of migraine perceptible?

Synaptic is an audiovisual installation that externalizes migraine sensations such as pulsing pressure, visual aura, light sensitivity, and perceptual distortion. Dynamic LED patterns move through layers of silkscreen-printed acrylic, producing shifting depth, glare, and points of intensity.

A live-coded Sonic Pi soundscape uses repetition, pitch, and density to evoke cycles of tension, sensory overload, and temporary release. Drawing on arts-based approaches to pain representation, the project treats visualization not as a cure, but as a tool for reflection, communication, and creating distance from an otherwise invisible experience.
