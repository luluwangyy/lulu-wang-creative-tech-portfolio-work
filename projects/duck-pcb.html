The Duck: Custom PCB Design & Fabrication

A hand-fabricated PCB pendant that turns a blinking red LED into a duck’s quack.

Overview

In my Creative Embedded Systems class, I used a component kit to design a PCB from scratch. I drew the schematic and board layout in KiCad, milled the board from copper-clad stock, and soldered the components by hand. The red LED sits at the duck’s bill, so each blink reads like a quack.

Circuit & PCB Design

The TL555 runs as an astable oscillator. Two resistors and a capacitor set its timing, and pin 3 drives the red LED through a current-limiting resistor. The blink comes from the circuit itself.

I drew the duck outline in Illustrator, imported the SVG into KiCad’s Edge.Cuts layer, and aligned the LED with the bill. I put the coin-cell holder on the back and used traces wider than 0.3 mm as a milling guideline.

Technical Specifications & Bill of Materials

Low-power CMOS 555 oscillator with coin-cell power and a single 5 mm red LED, fabricated on CNC-milled copper-clad stock. The design files include the KiCad schematic and board, Gerber layers, and plated and non-plated drill exports.

Reproduction Steps

  1. Build the TL555 astable schematic in KiCad and wire the output to the LED through a current-limiting resistor.
  2. Import the outline, place the LED at the bill and battery holder at the rear, then route the board.
  3. Export Gerber and drill files from KiCad. Convert the Gerber artwork to SVG and import it into Easel.
  4. Secure the copper blank on the Carvey bed and mill the traces and outline. The build notes record a trace cut depth of roughly 0.11–0.15 mm.
  5. Clean up the edges, solder the components with the timer last, then power the board and check that the LED blinks.

Lessons & Improvements

I placed a milling tab near the bill. Removing it meant filing right against the smallest curves on the board. On the next revision, I’d move the tabs to the flatter back or bottom edge so I can remove them without reshaping the bill.
