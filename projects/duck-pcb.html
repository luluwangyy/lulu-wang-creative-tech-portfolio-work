The Duck: Custom PCB Design & Fabrication

A hand-fabricated PCB pendant that turns a blinking red LED into a duck’s quack.

Overview

In my Creative Embedded Systems class, I started with a kit of components and designed a printed circuit board from scratch. I drew the schematic and laid out the board, milled it from copper-clad stock, and soldered the components by hand.

I wanted the circuit itself to feel playful. I shaped the board like a duck and put the red LED at its bill, so each flash reads like a quack.

Circuit & PCB Design

I drew the duck’s outline in Illustrator and imported the SVG into KiCad’s Edge.Cuts layer. I placed the LED at the bill, moved the coin-cell holder to the back, and routed the copper traces with the limits of CNC milling in mind.

A TL555 timer runs in astable mode. The resistors and capacitor set the blink rate, and the timer’s output drives the red LED.

Milling & Assembly

CNC milling

I exported Gerber and drill files from KiCad, converted the Gerber artwork to SVG for Easel, and milled the traces and outline on a Carvey CNC.

Hand soldering

After milling, I cleaned up the board edges and soldered the components onto the PCB, leaving the TL555 until last. I powered the assembled board with a coin cell to check the blink.

Technical Stack

  • Circuit: TL555 CMOS timer, resistors, a timing capacitor, red LED, and coin-cell power.
  • Design: Adobe Illustrator for the board outline and KiCad for the schematic, placement, routing, and fabrication exports.
  • Fabrication: Gerber and drill files, Gerber-to-SVG conversion, Easel, and Carvey CNC milling.

The blink is generated by the timer circuit itself.

Reflection

I placed a milling tab too close to the bill. Filing it off without flattening the small curves was harder than routing the outline on screen. On the next board, I’d put the tabs on the flatter back or bottom edge.
